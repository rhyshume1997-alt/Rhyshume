# Designing the character

A mascot is a promise with a face. Fun is the wrapper; the job is to make people **feel the app's promise** every time it shows up: on the first screen, when it's empty, while they wait, when it works, when it breaks.

## 1. Find the meaning (one sentence)

Write: **"<Name> is a <thing> that <does/has something>: <the promise>."**

- Penny is a piggy bank with a sprout growing from its coin slot: your savings, growing.
- Shelly is a turtle whose shell is a shield with a keyhole: your passwords, kept safe.
- Ember is a little flame: your streak, alive as long as you keep it going.

How to get there:
1. **The promise**, from the interview: what does the person feel when the app works? (safe, proud, calm, clever, unstoppable, looked after).
2. **The domain object**: the thing that already means the app's job (coin, cloud, page, clock, leaf, key, plate, map pin, speech bubble, note, battery, droplet).
3. **Bend one into the other**: give the object a creature's body, or a creature one feature of the object. The meaning sits in **one** detail (the sprout, the keyhole, the moon tuft): the signature feature.
4. **Check the cliché**: a lightbulb for ideas, a robot for every AI app, a generic blob with a smile for anything. If it's the first thing anyone would draw, keep the domain but twist it (a robot is fine if its antenna is the app's thing; a blob is fine for calm, abstract brands, where softness IS the meaning).

## 2. Shape language

The silhouette carries the personality before the face does.

| Shape | Reads as | Good for |
|---|---|---|
| Circles, pebbles, soft blobs | friendly, safe, calm | money, health, wellness, kids, sleep |
| Squares, sturdy rectangles | dependable, solid, honest | security, finance, productivity, tools |
| Triangles, points, flames | energy, speed, edge | fitness, streaks, games, dev tools |
| Tall and thin | elegant, clever, a bit aloof | luxury, editorial, design tools |
| Wide and low | grounded, relaxed, cosy | food, home, journaling |

- One big shape for the body, at most two smaller ones (ears, a tuft, a tail). If you need a fourth shape to explain it, cut one.
- **Big head or round body, short limbs**: appeal comes from proportion (head ≥ 40% of the height for cute; smaller head for cooler, older characters).
- **Face low and wide** on the head = young and warm. Face high and close = older, sharper.
- **Silhouette test**: filled black at 64 px, can you still tell what it is? If not, the signature feature is too small or inside the silhouette (move it to the edge: ears, tuft, antenna, handle).

## 3. Colour

- **One body colour** carries the brand. If the app's accent is strong, use it; if the app is mostly that colour already, pick a neighbour so the mascot pops against the UI (a coral mascot on a blue app, a lime one on charcoal).
- Details in one second colour at most (the sprout's green, the backpack, the headphones). Eyes dark, cheeks a soft blush.
- Avoid: purple-to-blue gradients on everything, grey "neutral" mascots on grey apps, five colours.

## 4. Personality → expression

The personality goes in the brief's `personality` and shows in the face and posture of every pose:
- **Gentle and warm**: soft closed smile, eyes slightly squinted, relaxed arms.
- **Cheeky and bold**: one-sided smile, a lean, a lifted brow.
- **Calm and wise**: half-closed eyes, still posture, small smile.
- **Hyper and playful**: open mouth, arms out, weight forward.

## 5. The name

1-2 syllables, says itself, fits the personality (soft sounds for gentle: Mochi, Luna; hard for bold: Volt, Byte). Not a trademark you know of, not the app's own name unless they ask (except when the mascot IS the brand). Offer it, don't ask for it; they can change it.

## 6. Three ideas, not three drawings

The concepts are three different answers to the same promise, so the person really chooses: three different objects or creatures, three silhouettes. Build them from different domain objects (step 1.2), or one object and two creatures. Never the same thing in three poses or three colours: that's a choice they can't make.

## 7. Write the brief

`character` is concrete and visual: shapes, parts, materials. Not adjectives ("cute", "friendly" do nothing; "a round piggy bank with a coin slot on its back" does everything). Name the signature feature again in `feature`. Name colours as materials ("warm coral pink", "deep moss green"), not hex codes.

## Judge a concept

Look at each concept and answer, honestly:
1. **Meaning**: could a stranger guess the app's world from the character alone?
2. **Silhouette**: one clear shape? Readable at 64 px?
3. **Signature feature**: there, big enough, at the edge of the silhouette?
4. **Appeal**: proportions (big head or round body, short limbs), face placement, would you want it on your phone?
5. **On style**: does it match the look (clay is matte and soft; plush has fabric and stitched eyes; line is flat with black outlines)?
6. **Clean**: no text or letters, no floor or backdrop, no cut-off parts, no extra limbs, one character.

Recommend the one that wins on 1-3, not the prettiest render. If two tie, the simpler silhouette wins.
