Down the aisle

Five ways to group the same objects. Scroll sideways, use the arrows, or press the arrow keys with the strip focused.

  1. A

    For the table

    Mugs, bowls, a tray and a stack of tea towels. The things that get used before anyone has sat down.

  2. B

    For a long evening

    Jar candles, a thick pillar and a pair of tapers. Set them out before the light goes.

  3. C

    For writing back

    Cards with a printed sun or a printed branch, and a notebook for what comes after.

  4. D

    For a bare windowsill

    A terracotta pot, a vase of dried stems and a tall cream vase. Green and otherwise.

  5. E

    For someone else

    Small things that wrap easily: a dish, a candle, a tea towel, a card.

Marginalia

Shop notes

Note 1

Why the objects are drawn

There are no product photographs in this concept, so each object is a flat drawing in three colours and their tints. On a live site every drawing would give way to a real photograph, on the same shelf, with the same turn-it-over view.

Margin: placeholders such as [price to be confirmed] mark where the owner's facts go.

Note 2

How the wall moves

Choose a category and the other objects lower and fade. The ones that match slide along their shelf to close the gap. It is the same thing a person does when they tidy a shelf around what you asked for.

Margin: with reduced motion switched on, the wall simply changes.

Note 3

What the basket is

A basket that holds objects, counts them and lets you change the amounts. It does not take payment, ask for an address or remember anything once the page closes.

Margin: the last step says so plainly.
