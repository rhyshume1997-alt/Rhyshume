Creative ideas proposed by Rhys

  • The dark, cinematic direction (about 70 percent Salt & Ember, 30 percent Little Kettle, as asked by Michael)
  • “Something good on the shelves”, “Explore the shop”, “Discover the deli” (proposed wording only)
  • The headline lighting letter by letter from the side nearest the pour
  • The framed enamel sign for the logo, and the scroll that carries the dark stage into the warm shelves over a wooden counter lip
  • The flavour dials that re-order sample drinks, ticket-stub deli cards, and services as one tabbed section with a small demonstration each
  • A small brass round stamp

Temporary parts

  • Logo: the client’s supplied image (a smiling bull with a barrel, “Jack O’Beers & Deli”) is used as sent, with its white background removed. It is a small 355 px copy, so a vector or high-resolution file is needed. The logo says “& Deli” while the workbook says “Jack O’Beers”: confirm the trading name.
  • Colours: provisional: deep forest green, dark mahogany, warm cream and amber. They are not taken from the client’s logo values, which are still to be confirmed.
  • Font: Dubai files were supplied, but the zip has no licence and the font data reads “© 2017 Dubai Executive Council. All rights reserved” (Monotype). Web use is unverified, so no Dubai file is stored in or served from this project. Interface text uses Dubai only where it is installed on the viewer’s computer (it ships with Windows 10 and 11), otherwise IBM Plex Sans. Headlines use Newsreader, which has no true italic here, so italics are the browser’s slanted version. Confirm the licence for web embedding before it goes on a live site.
  • Imagery: the pour footage and the bottle photograph are AI-generated references, not client photography. The bottle stands in for every beer and cider.
  • Products, flavours, pairings, categories: all sample data, labelled Demo. Prices and allergens show as to be confirmed.

Missing content and assets

  • Logo as vector or high-resolution file, plus any variants and brand colour values
  • Shop photography (stock and the shop itself), and professional video, as the workbook says more is needed
  • Existing photos and videos the client already has
  • Product list with names, descriptions, flavour notes, pairings, ABV, sizes and verified allergen data
  • Shop address, opening hours, contact details, about text, deli range
  • Copy for every page, and who writes it
  • Domain login and Shopify access (to be arranged properly, never by sharing a password in a message)

Functional requirements needing agreement

  • Online purchasing: which products are sold online, and whether the till and shop stock stay in step
  • Click and collect: slots, lead time, stock, who is allowed to collect
  • Shipping: areas, carriers, packaging, cost rules, alcohol delivery restrictions
  • Hampers: fixed or build-your-own, pricing, packaging
  • Equipment rental: what “keyser units” means, what is rented, the notes about two kegs, charging “for what they use” and customers choosing their dates. None of this is assumed. Needs a conversation before anything is built.
  • Membership: what members get, accounts, personal data, sign-up
  • Product pages: who supplies and keeps flavour, food and allergen information up to date

Decisions before a quote and formal specification

  1. Shopify theme or separate front end (see below)
  2. Which of the requested services are in the first release
  3. Logo, colours and font confirmed; trading name confirmed
  4. Who supplies photography, video, product data and copy
  5. How age is checked and how alcohol sales and delivery are handled, once the client has confirmed their licensing position
  6. What the till integration needs to do, if anything
  7. Domain, hosting and ongoing care arrangements

Shopify implementation considerations

The client already lists Shopify. A separate custom storefront is not assumed to be the best answer.

Customised Shopify theme

  • Keeps checkout, accounts, orders, tax, shipping and stock in one place the client may already use
  • Most of this design (layout, type, colour, flavour pages, deli cards, the opening animation) can be built in a theme with product metafields for flavour, pairing and allergen data
  • Click and collect and shipping are standard Shopify features, subject to plan and apps
  • Limits: the lying-to-standing opening and the cursor are front-end work inside theme code, fine but needs care for speed; rental and membership logic usually need apps

Separate front end on Shopify’s APIs

  • More freedom for animation and bespoke flows
  • More to build, host and maintain; checkout and account behaviour need extra care; the client depends on a developer for changes
  • Worth it only if the theme cannot deliver something the client truly needs

Leaning (to confirm): start with a customised theme. Dependencies: Shopify plan and store status (the workbook says no website, yet lists Shopify, so what exists is unconfirmed), access arranged safely, DNA till integration options, and the client’s alcohol compliance answers. No Shopify account was connected, and no data, orders or credentials were touched.

Rev 4 direction: cinematic deli

  • About 70 percent Salt & Ember (dark photographic stage, oversized serif, numbered sections, a scroll that carries the stage into the shop) and about 30 percent Little Kettle (rounded corners where they help, ticket-stub deli cards, friendly copy, a small brass stamp).
  • Palette is provisional: deep forest green, dark mahogany, warm cream and amber. It is not taken from the client’s logo values, which are still to be confirmed.
  • The opening is dark. The logo is on a framed enamel sign that lies on the counter, stands up and is lit by the room. It uses the client’s supplied file as it is. No official branding was invented.
  • The catalogue stays on a light, readable surface. Not every section is dark: the flavour finder and the closing band are, the shelves, deli and services are not.
  • For an adult alcohol audience: no children, no cartoon characters, no claims, no encouragement to drink more, an age notice, and the compliance questions still open. Treat this as a design check, not legal advice.

Imagery and media

  • The bottle photograph and pour footage are AI-generated references, not client photography. Any branding or text inside the originals is invented and is not used.
  • The footage is a short, low-resolution clip, and the bottle cut-out stands in for every beer and cider. Real product photography and a purpose-made film are needed (see the asset specification in the project documents).
  • Commercial-use terms for the generated media have not been checked.
  • See the prototype lab for the drawn and photographic versions side by side.

Known technical and compliance risks

  • Alcohol: licensing conditions, age verification, delivery restrictions, permitted payment and sale processes, advertising standards, privacy and accounts all need checking with the client and their advisers. Nothing here claims any set-up satisfies Scottish licensing.
  • Marketing: the audience includes young families, but alcohol promotion must not target or appeal to children. The visuals avoid child-focused imagery.
  • Product information: allergens and ABV must come from verified sources
  • AI-generated references stand in for real photography and film; real imagery changes the feel and the page weight
  • Heavy animation and video must stay fast on phones; reduced motion is respected
  • Till and Shopify stock sync is an unknown
  • Dubai font licence unchecked
  • The preview lives in the Rhys Hume repository under an unguessable, noindex address. It is private by obscurity only, not password protected.
