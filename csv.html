How validation runs

The validation lifecycle

Six steps, from the plan to the periodic review

  1. CSV planning

    Service: CSV

    The old way: Validation is an afterthought, with no agreed plan for what to test

    With the system: A validation plan scoped to the system, its intended use and your quality system

    The outcome: Everyone agrees what validated means before testing starts

  2. Risk assessment

    Service: CSV

    The old way: Everything is tested equally, or only what is easy to test

    With the system: Risk-based thinking shows where testing effort needs to go

    The outcome: Effort goes where it matters for product quality and data integrity

  3. IQ/OQ/PQ protocols

    Service: CSV

    The old way: Testing is informal and the results are scattered

    With the system: Protocols are written and executed, with results recorded as the testing happens

    The outcome: A clear record of what was tested and what happened

  4. Traceability matrix

    Service: CSV

    The old way: It's hard to show that every requirement was tested

    With the system: Requirements are linked to tests, so nothing falls through the gaps

    The outcome: Coverage you can show to an auditor

  5. Validation report

    Service: CSV

    The old way: The outcome is spread across documents and emails

    With the system: A clear summary of what was tested, what was found and the outcome

    The outcome: One report your QA team can review

  6. Periodic review

    Service: CSV

    The old way: Systems drift away from how they are actually used, and nobody notices

    With the system: Validated systems are checked against how they are used

    The outcome: Systems stay in a validated state as things change
