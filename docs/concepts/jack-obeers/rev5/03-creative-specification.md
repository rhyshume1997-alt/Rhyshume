# Rev 5: creative specification

## A. Visual identity
Independent British drinks and deli: a corner shop with a deli counter, not a cocktail bar. Materials: dark mahogany wood and a worn counter lip, warm cream paper, forest-green enamel tiles, brass trim, amber beer light. Provisional palette (sampled from the logo, forest added): mahogany `#1d0f0a`, wood `#4a2416`, forest `#12291d`, cream `#f2e8d2`, paper `#f8f1df`, amber `#d9992b`, brass `#b98a3e`. Red is not used. Type: Newsreader display at very large sizes with an italic amber accent phrase; Source Sans 3 text (Dubai stand-in); IBM Plex Mono small labels. Shading: soft contact shadows under objects, a faint paper grain and wood grain, no neon glow.

## B. Opening composition
Dark stage. A very large headline "Fresh from keg to bottle, *no foam, no fuss.*" on the left. On the right a tall **arch window** holding the pour film (the bottle enters from the top edge of the arch, so it is cropped by the frame and never floats). Below, a worn wooden counter lip. The logo medallion (round enamel sign, never a square box) lies on its side on the counter.

## C. Signature interaction
1. **Opening beat (about 3 s):** the medallion gets up from lying down while the pour starts. The headline lights letter by letter, tied to the pour's progress, so the beer filling the glass is what "writes" the line.
2. **Headbutt:** pressing "Come in" (or scrolling) makes the medallion lunge and the arch expand to fill the screen, carrying the dark stage into the cream shop (the new page).
3. **The beer cursor (drinks shelf only):** a weighted brown bottle follows the mouse over the shelf, tips toward a product's glass and fills it in that beer's colour. It hides itself over text and controls. Tap, keyboard and reduced-motion versions fill the glass without the bottle.

## D. How the opening becomes the website
Pinned arch expansion: scroll drives the arch from window to full bleed, the headline fades, the counter lip slides up and becomes the edge of the cream "Shelf" section. The header and Shop button stay available the whole time.

## E. Product discovery
"The Shelf" has big numbered index rows (01 Beers, 02 Ciders, 03 Wines, 04 Kegs) that filter the shelf. Each product is a glass on a wooden shelf with its name tag. Selecting one opens a **label panel**: descriptor, flavour dials, food pairings, allergens (to be confirmed), Add to basket. That is the two-click route: row or header (1), product (2).

## F. Supporting compositions
- **Deli:** full-bleed forest band with a scalloped awning edge; ticket-stub list that swaps a large photo slot on hover or focus (Salt & Ember course list idea).
- **Hampers:** cream band, arch photo slot, two sample hampers, contents to be agreed.
- **Services:** numbered index rows that open (click and collect, shipping, keg and keezer hire, members), each honest about what is unconfirmed.
- **Find us:** dark counter band with address, hours, directions.

## G. Desktop and mobile
Desktop: two-column hero, arch window right, side-by-side shelf. Mobile: headline first, arch below at 4:5, the shelf as a horizontal snap row of glasses, the cursor replaced by tap-to-pour, label panel as a bottom sheet.

## I. What makes it recognisably Jack O'Beers
The bull medallion that lies down and gets up; the pour as the headline's pen; the glass-on-the-shelf product model with a bottle that pours it; deli ticket stubs; a shop that is open Wednesday to Monday, shown honestly.
