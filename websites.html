1.0 Scope

Buyers search first and ask later. If your firm is hard to find, or your site is awkward on a phone with no clear way to ask for a quote, the enquiry goes elsewhere.

I build the front of the system. That’s the site that gets found, the form that takes the enquiry and the drawing, and the profile that collects reviews.

2.0 What I build

2.1Mobile-friendly sites
They read well on a phone in a workshop or a van, and just as well on a desktop.
2.2Quote request forms
A clear form asks what you’d ask on the phone, takes the drawing if you want it to, and sends the request to your email.
2.3Local search set-up
SEO and your Google Business Profile, so people searching for your kind of work nearby can find you.
2.4Readable by machines
Clear page content and structured data, so search engines and AI assistants can read what you make.
2.5Reviews and repeat work
A Google Business Profile and a review link make it easy to leave a review.

3.0 How quote requests come through

Each enquiry arrives as one clear message. The questions you need answered before you price a job are already asked, so there is less back and forth.

I agree the questions with you in the proposal, so the form fits the way you quote.

4.0 What you get

  • A mobile-friendly website built around how your customers contact you.
  • A quote request form that asks what you need to price a job, with the request sent to your email.
  • Local search set-up, including SEO and your Google Business Profile.
  • Clear page content and structured data, so search engines and AI assistants can read what you make.
  • A written proposal before any work starts.

The form asks the questions you need and sends each enquiry straight to your email, to help fewer enquiries slip through.

Engineering and manufacturing firms: fabrication, machining and trade businesses, mostly in and around Glasgow.
